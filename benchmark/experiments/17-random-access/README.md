# 17 random access

2026-09-13. the compression tables say what each profile saves. this one asks what a reader pays to get one card back out, because a file nobody can open one card from is not a dataset, it is an archive.

hypothesis: the single av1 stream pays for its ratio at read time. pulling one card means seeking into a 38627-frame video and decoding to the frame, which should cost more per card than the per-image blobs (avif, jxl) that decode in O(1) with nothing before them; the retrieval and stills profiles are cheap on disk and expensive to open.
method: benchmark/tools/measure_latency.py over the four candidate files on disk, 200 item ordinals drawn once with default_rng(7) and reused for every file; per card, the forge read path as it exists in the urna checkout: blob lookup in the mmap, the decoder as a child process (ffmpeg with -ss for the stream, avifdec and djxl for the blobs), the rgb array back in python; p50/p90/p99 over the 200 cards; the av1 stream also through decode_frames_at with 10 hits per ffmpeg; the process-spawn floor as 50 runs of each binary with --version; one run on the quiet machine on 2026-09-12 and one pair (before and after urna pr #138) on 2026-09-13 with the five-model build holding the gpu.
verdict: refuted. on the quiet machine the av1 stream was the cheapest read of the four, 27 ms p50 at crf50 and 29 ms at crf35, against 36 ms for the jxl blob and 70 ms for avif; with keyint=1 every frame is a keyframe, so the seek lands on the card and there is no walk, and 23 of those 27 ms are ffmpeg starting up. the per-image backends were slow for a reason that had nothing to do with the codec: the read path asked the decoder for a png and paid libpng's deflate on every card (127 of avifdec's 145 ms). urna #138 writes the intermediate uncompressed (avif) or as ppm (jxl) and the same cards under the same load went 94 to 32 ms (avif) and 43 to 16 ms (jxl-transcode). the batched stream path was worse than broken: it raised on every call whose last hit was not the last frame (fixed in #138), and once it returns it costs 239 ms per frame for 10 random hits because it decodes everything between the first and the last one.

i went in expecting the video container to be the thing that made the small profiles hard to use, and i had the story ready: seek, walk to a keyframe, decode, pay for the ratio. the numbers said the opposite on the first run. all-intra is what saves it. there is no keyframe walk because every frame is one, so the seek is a byte offset and the decode is one 488x680 frame, a few milliseconds of dav1d. what is left is the cost of starting ffmpeg, and that is the same 23 ms whether the file is 533 MB or 1.3 GB.

the per-image numbers bothered me more. avif was supposed to be the O(1) backend and it was the slowest read of the four. it took one card, one avif blob and eight invocations of avifdec to see it: default png output, 145 ms; `--png-compress 0`, 18 ms. same pixels, the tests assert it. djxl had the same tax at a smaller rate, 30 ms to png against 11 ms to ppm. none of this was the codec. it was the two lines that turned the decoder's output into a numpy array, written for correctness on a twelve-frame test corpus and never timed on a real one.

the batched path is the part i am least happy about. `decode_frames_at` is meant to resolve k search hits in one ffmpeg run, and on the test stream it does. on the real stream it raised a broken-pipe error on every call, because the test's last hit happened to be the last frame of its twelve and on 38627 frames it never is; ffmpeg kept decoding past the span, filled the pipe and died on the close. that is fixed. what is not fixed, and is now written down, is that walking from the first hit to the last one is the wrong shape for hits spread over a corpus: 10 random cards span about 19000 frames and the walk costs 2.4 s, so ten single seeks at 36 ms each win by a factor of six. it is the right call for hits that sit close together, which is what the clustered neardup ordering produces and the stills ordering does not.

the machine was not quiet for the second run. the wemm build had the gpu and 15.5 of 16 GB of swap, the stills stream took 58 s to export where the quiet machine took 1.8, and the p99s are noisy. i kept the before and after pair because they were taken minutes apart under the same load and the ratio is what the fix claims; the quiet-machine after numbers come when the build ends.

provenance: apple m4, ffmpeg 9.0.1 with svt-av1, libavif 1.4.2 with dav1d 1.5.4, cjxl and djxl 0.12.0, urna checkout at #137 for the before run and the #138 branch for the after run. raw json under `data/`; results.json is assembled from the three latency files and the spawn floor.
