# 15 models over crf

2026-09-12. experiment 14 showed that clip cannot see a codec step on the name ruler because clip does not read the name. this one asks the models that do.

hypothesis: the three models that read the printed card text (siglip2, jina-v5-omni-nano@256, wemm-2b@256) lose more text-to-image utility under lossy media than clip, because av1 at crf50 blurs the title band that they key on while the art that clip keys on survives; at n=200 the loss shows up as a txt@1 delta beyond one pooled binomial standard error on at least one of them, and drift p10 walks down more for the text readers than for clip.
method: three forge builds of the same evenly spaced --sample 512 of the mtgdataset corpus (specs/15-lossless.toml archive profile jxl-transcode, specs/15-crf35.toml and specs/15-crf50.toml av1 tune still speed 6 all-intra fixed crf, no gate), each with potion text default plus the four image spaces clip-vit-b32, siglip2, jina-v5-omni-nano@256 and wemm-2b@256, embed_media = true, image_input = decoded_media so every stored vector is of the decoded frame; urna_model_bench.py from the urna forge checkout on each .urna, one process per preset (--models), --queries 200 --seed 7 -k 1 5 10, ruler "artwork of the card {label}" against each model's own image space, hits matched by chunk_id; the same 200 query items in every cell because the rng draws over the same 512 items; raw json under bench/<variant>.<preset>.json; se(txt@1) = sqrt(p(1-p)/200) per cell and z = delta / pooled se in the second table.
verdict: refuted. none of the four models loses txt@1 at crf50 beyond noise: clip 0.220 to 0.230 (+0.010, se 0.029, z 0.24), siglip2 0.920 to 0.930 (+0.010, se 0.019, z 0.38), jina 0.630 to 0.655 (+0.025, se 0.034, z 0.52), wemm-2b 0.925 to 0.910 (-0.015, se 0.020, z -0.55); the largest move at any k is clip txt@10 -0.035 and wemm txt@5 -0.020, both under two se. crf35 is flat too (deltas +0.020 to -0.005). drift p10 does move, and in the opposite direction to the hypothesis: crf50 costs clip 0.063 and siglip2 0.074 of cosine but jina only 0.025 and wemm 0.018, so the text readers are the ones whose embedding shifts least under the codec. media goes 48.2 MB to 17.8 MB to 6.6 MB (8.2x on the 53.8 MB jpeg source at crf50) and siglip2 is the only model that drops identity@1 below 1.000 (0.995 at crf50). on this sample the crf50 retrieval recipe is safe for the text-reading models: siglip2 and wemm-2b still find the card by name at 0.93 and 0.91 txt@1 on a file 7.3x smaller than the lossless one.

what i expected was the title band going soft and the name readers falling off a cliff, the way a human stops being able to read small print in a crushed jpeg. that is not what the vectors say. wemm-2b at 256 dims moves by 0.018 of cosine between the lossless frame and the crf50 frame, clip by 0.063, so a model that has to decode glyphs is holding on to a more stable representation of the card than a model that only sees texture and color. my reading is that the name is large, high contrast and sits on a flat band, which is exactly the content av1 protects at any crf, and what crf50 destroys is the fine grain in the art, which is what clip and siglip2's global pooling actually consume. siglip2 is the interesting one: it reads the name (0.92 txt@1 against clip's 0.22) but drifts like clip, which says its image tower is still mostly an art embedding with a name signal riding on top.

n=200 is the ceiling of what this run can say. one query is 0.005 of txt@1 and the pooled se of a difference sits at 0.026 to 0.048 depending on the cell, so a model would have to lose 0.05 to 0.10 absolute before this bench could call it. none did, and the sign of the crf50 delta is positive for three of four, which is what noise looks like. the sample also has a ceiling on the ruler side: siglip2 and wemm-2b are at 0.98 txt@5, so on the name query there is almost nothing left to lose on 512 cards; a name-collision-heavy slice (reprints, split cards, tokens) or the full corpus would be the place to look for a real loss. the experiment 03 numbers on a 1500-card sample (siglip2 0.850, wemm 0.933, jina 0.617, clip 0.233 txt@1) line up with the lossless row here, so the sample is not a friendly one.

throughput is the cost that did move. wemm-2b embeds the 512 decoded frames at 0.61 items per second on crf35 and 0.65 on crf50 (the lossless row was measured at 0.41 while another forge chain shared the gpu for part of it), jina at 1.51 and 1.86, siglip2 at 16 and 25, clip at 36 and 49. the higher rates on crf50 are probably decode side, smaller av1 frames decode faster and the embed timing includes the decode, but i did not isolate that. a full-corpus wemm-2b build stays at the 17 to 26 hour estimate of experiment 03 regardless of the codec.

failures: the first lossless build died at wemm-2b with rc 4 (no model.safetensors in the hub snapshot) because the 5.4 GB weight file from the previous session's download was still an .incomplete blob; the file landed nine minutes later, the crf35 and crf50 builds picked it up, and the lossless build was rerun with --resume, which reused the media stage and the cached clip, siglip2 and jina vectors and only ran wemm-2b (1254 s). the siglip2 bench failed once on its text tower: open_clip asks transformers for the hf tokenizer of timm/ViT-B-16-SigLIP2, the forge sets the hub offline on import, and offline transformers raises on the repo's missing config.json instead of skipping it; the bench was rerun with the offline vars set to 0 explicitly (the tokenizer files were already in the cache, no download happened). the bench also needs URNA_ALLOW_REMOTE_CODE=jina-v5-omni-nano,wemm-2b, the manifest is not an authorization.

provenance: measured on 2026-09-12 on apple silicon mps fp16, forge checkout nest-wt-main. builds 1263 s (crf35), 1126 s (crf50), 403 s plus 1273 s (lossless, two attempts); bench per preset on lossless, crf35, crf50: clip 8, 21, 9 s; siglip2 17, 47, 30 s; jina 94, 115, 99 s; wemm-2b 461, 318, 277 s. the three .urna files are 49.4, 19.0 and 7.8 MB under `benchmark/runs/15-{lossless,crf35,crf50}/` with their full manifest sidecars; results.json is assembled from those manifests and the twelve bench files by assemble.py.
