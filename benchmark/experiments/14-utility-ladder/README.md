# 14 utility ladder

2026-09-12. the same question as experiment 13, ten times the queries: does the text-to-image hit rate move when the codec gets worse, and can n=1000 see it.

hypothesis: the n=100 txt@k table of experiment 13 was sampling noise; at n=1000 the lossless archive, neardup, avif q48, crf40 and crf50 either separate on the clip name ruler or land inside one binomial standard error of each other, and either outcome settles what a retrieval-only gate can measure with this ruler.
method: urna_model_bench.py from the urna forge checkout (commit cbc326de) on the five full-corpus .urna files under `candidates/`, each with its full manifest sidecar, --queries 1000 --seed 7 -k 1 5 10 and the ruler "artwork of the card {label}" in the clip-vit-b32 space, hits matched by chunk_id; the three tiers reported separately (identity@k from source-image queries, drift cosine p10, txt@k); raw bench json kept under `bench/`; se(txt@k) = sqrt(p(1-p)/n) and z = (txt@1 - lossless txt@1) / pooled se; the n=100 numbers of experiment 13 placed beside the n=1000 ones for the same files.
verdict: confirmed for everything down to crf50, and the ladder does have a floor: txt@1 at n=1000 is 0.092 lossless, 0.096 neardup, 0.096 avif q48, 0.089 crf40, 0.093 crf50, 0.074 crf55 and 0.069 crf60, se about 0.009 each. crf50 sits +0.001 from lossless (z 0.08) and avif q48 +0.004 (z 0.31), indistinguishable from the archive; crf55 is -0.018 (z -1.5) and crf60 is -0.023 (z -1.9) with identity@1 down to 0.971: from crf55 on the hit rate itself starts to move, and at crf60 it is two standard errors under lossless. so on this ruler the retrieval-only recipe holds through crf50 and the edge sits between 50 and 55. drift p10 walks down the whole way (0.9968 to 0.8866) and never says where the edge is; a retrieval-only gate needs the hit@k floor, and with clip it can only see a step this large, so the stronger rulers of experiment 15 are what make it precise.

what n did is the whole finding. at n=100 the cells carried se 0.022 to 0.029, and the 0.040 range across files was about one pooled standard error; at n=1000 the range is 0.007. drift p10 moved by at most 0.004 between the two draws, so the cosine statistic was already stable at n=100 and only the hit rate was not. the ruler itself is the ceiling: clip-vit-b32 sees the art and not the printed name, 0.09 is what it can do on any of these files, and a codec would have to remove more than a quarter of that before this test could tell. the models that read the name (siglip2 0.850, wemm-2b 0.933 txt@1 on the 1500 sample of experiment 03) are the ones a gate should query, or real operator queries with expected ids through the bench's --queries-file tier. crf50 is 0.533 GB, 7.46x on the .urna, visually bad (experiment 13), and on this ruler indistinguishable from 3.6 GB of lossless jxl. crf55 and crf60 were built and measured right after the first five, same command; the stills file (crf35, no clustering) is the neardup media in a single stream and was not benched separately.

provenance: measured on 2026-09-12, one bench process per file on apple silicon mps while a forge build chain ran on the same machine, 34 to 55 s per file (archive 55.3, neardup 42.5, crf40 34.4, crf50 33.9, avif q48 36.1). the archive and neardup candidates are byte-identical to `release/v0.3/{archive,neardup}/mtgdataset.urna` (sha256 matches the release SHA256SUMS); the release-shaped manifest.json without items was not needed because every candidate dir carries the full sidecar.
